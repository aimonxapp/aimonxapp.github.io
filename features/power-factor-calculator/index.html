Updated November 9, 2026

Rulebooks change, and yours has the final word. These floors come from the USPSA Competition Rules (September 2026) and IPSC's handgun division rules.

How it's calculated

Power Factor is bullet weight in grains times muzzle velocity in feet per second, divided by 1,000. The decimals get dropped, not rounded up: 124.9 is 124, not 125. A 124-grain bullet at 1,050 fps comes out at 130.

Muzzle energy shows in foot-pounds when you enter fps, and in joules when you enter m/s.

Your number on paper, and at the chrono

The calculator gives you your number on paper. At a USPSA match, the chrono decides: you fire three rounds, the average velocity counts, and your bullet gets weighed on the match scale. If you come up short, you get a few more rounds to make it. Miss the major floor and your match is scored minor. Miss the minor floor and you can keep shooting, but not for score.

What does each round cost you?

Loading for major? Work out what your recipe costs per round, and print a load card for your bench, with our reloading cost calculator.

Save your setups

Give a setup a name, like "124 gr match load, 5-inch barrel", and save it to the history. AIMONX keeps your last 50, newest first, with weight, velocity, Power Factor, energy, date and time.

The history is a scratch pad: it isn't part of your logbook, your backup or your archive. Once it's gone, it's gone.

On every ammo card too

Add the muzzle velocity to any ammo you track, factory or handloaded, and its Power Factor and energy stay on its card for good.

Free

The calculator on this page is free for everyone, and so is the one in the app, along with the ballistics on your ammo cards. No limits.
